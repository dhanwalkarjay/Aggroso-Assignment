Community Climate Action Grant Guidelines

The Community Climate Action Grant supports neighborhood projects that reduce household energy use, improve resilience, or expand practical climate education. Applicants may request between $10,000 and $50,000 for projects completed within twelve months of the award date. The program favors clear, measurable activities that can be delivered by local organizations with meaningful resident participation.

Eligibility and applicant information

The applicant must be a registered nonprofit organization, public institution, or fiscally sponsored community group operating in the county. The application must identify the organization’s legal name, primary contact, mailing address, and fiscal sponsor when applicable. Applicants shall describe the neighborhood or communities served and explain how residents helped shape the proposed project.

Project description and work plan

The application must describe the problem being addressed, the project activities, the people responsible for delivery, and a schedule with major milestones. Applicants must state the number of residents expected to participate and explain how participation will be tracked. The work plan must identify at least one measurable outcome related to energy savings, preparedness, or climate education.

Budget and required attachment

Applicants must provide a complete project budget that separates personnel, materials, travel, contracted services, and indirect costs. Each budget category must include a brief justification. Requested grant funds must be connected to the activities and schedule in the application. A budget narrative is required when any single category exceeds $10,000.

The application shall include a signed letter of organizational authorization. Applications without this required attachment will be considered incomplete for review. The letter must name the person authorized to accept grant funds and confirm that the information in the application is accurate.

Evaluation and learning

Applicants should explain how they will collect information about activities and outcomes. They should identify a simple method for recording attendance, completed installations, lessons delivered, or other relevant measures. Applicants are encouraged to describe how project learning will be shared with residents and partner organizations. A short reflection at the end of the project is preferred but is not required.

Submission details

The application must be submitted by 5:00 p.m. local time on September 30, 2026. Late applications will not be reviewed in this grant cycle. Applicants should use plain language and define technical terms for a general community audience. Questions about these guidelines may be directed to the program office before submission.

Awardees should retain basic project records, attendance information, and spending documentation for at least three years after the project ends. The program office may request these records during a learning review.
